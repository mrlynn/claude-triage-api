# Intro video — camera script

For the landing page hero. Read to camera, no teleprompter cadence — the
sentences are short on purpose so you can look up between them.

Two cuts below. The **main cut** runs about 2:10 at a normal speaking pace.
The **short cut** is 45 seconds for social, and it is not a trim of the main
one — it drops the course entirely and sells the demo.

Delivery notes are in brackets. Don't read them.

---

## Main cut (~2:10)

**[Cold open. No greeting, no name yet. Look straight down the lens.]**

A support queue is where a company finds out it hurt someone.

**[Beat. Let that sit.]**

Most tickets are a late package or a wrong size. But the injury report
arrives in the same inbox, written in the same tone, often with the same
subject line. Somebody has to notice.

Northwind Outfitters is a company I made up. The numbers are not made up,
because they came from real support orgs. Forty-one hundred tickets a week.
Eleven thousand three hundred in December. Thirty-four agents, and in peak
season a third of them started last Tuesday.

Here's the one that matters. A parent writes in. Their kid got sick, and the
bottle liner had been flaking. The message opens with "probably nothing," so
the keyword layer sees no defect words and drops it in the general queue.

It sat there three days.

**[Slow down. Don't editorialize — just say it.]**

That's the whole reason this course exists. You're going to build the service
that catches that message.

**[Shift up. Brisker from here.]**

Four routes. Triage gives you structured outputs, so the model's contract is
your type system. Resolve gives you tool use, so Claude queries your systems
and shows its work. Draft gives you streaming. Estimate gives you token
counting, so you know the bill before you pay it.

Each route adds exactly one idea and builds on the one before it. One
codebase you can read end to end. Solutions for every lab.

And you don't have to commit to any of that to see it work. Go to the shop,
file a support ticket about whatever you want, and watch a live model
categorize it, rank the urgency, and decide whether a person needs to see it.
Nothing to install. No key. Then try to talk it out of doing its job — there's
a playground for that, and some of it works.

**[Settle. Lower. This is the close.]**

The techniques here are ordinary. You'll use them on something less serious
and they'll be the same techniques. But the reason to get them right only
shows up when the cost of being wrong is a person.

Link's below. Start with sixty seconds if that's what you have.

## Short cut (~45s)

**[No preamble.]**

This is a shop I built for a company that doesn't exist.

Pick a jacket. File a complaint about it. Say anything you want.

**[Beat.]**

A live model reads what you wrote, categorizes it, ranks how urgent it is,
and decides whether a human needs to see it. That last part is the one that
matters — the whole thing exists because a real support queue once sat on a
child's injury report for three days, and the message that reported it opened
with "probably nothing."

Nothing to install. No key. Sixty seconds.

Then, if you want to build it yourself, the course is right there.

## On-screen / B-roll cues

| Beat | What's on screen |
|---|---|
| "A support queue is where…" | You, full frame. Nothing else. |
| "Northwind Outfitters is a company I made up" | Storefront home, tent at sunrise |
| The numbers | The volume table, or just the numbers typed large |
| "probably nothing" | The ticket itself, that phrase highlighted |
| "It sat there three days" | Hold on the ticket. Silence. |
| Four routes | The route table, one row appearing per capability |
| "Go to the shop" | Screen recording: typing a ticket, result coming back |
| "try to talk it out of doing its job" | The injection playground |
| Close | You again, then the URL |

## If you need it shorter

Cut the paragraph starting "Four routes." The stakes and the demo carry the
video on their own; the capability list is the part a reader will get from the
page anyway.

## Things to say if someone asks on camera

- Northwind is invented, the shapes are not. Say it early — it buys credibility
  and saves the question later.
